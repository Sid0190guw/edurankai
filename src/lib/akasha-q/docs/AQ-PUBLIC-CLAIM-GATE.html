AQ-PUBLIC-CLAIM-GATE — Claim approval procedure and forms

SCOPE: WHAT COUNTS AS A CLAIM

A claim is any statement about Akasha-Q — its architecture, its evidence, its schedule, its relationship to Visvambhara, or its position on the six-rung reach ladder — made to any reader or listener outside the immediate working group. Surface does not change the definition. A slide title, a figure caption, a sentence in a funding memo, a spoken answer in a demonstration, a line in a job description and a caption under a photograph are all claims and all pass the same gate. Internal working notes are not claims; the moment a working note is shown outside the group it becomes one.

The gate decides one question only: may this exact sentence be said, at this evidence level, under this assumption set, on this surface, now. It does not decide whether the underlying engineering is a good idea, and it does not restrict what may be built. It restricts only what may be asserted.

THE FIVE DECISION STATES

The gate deliberately reuses the decision grammar of L5 command authority. Q1 who submitted this, Q2 is the trace valid, Q3 is this surface authorised for this claim now, Q4 should it be said at all given the current evidence state. Q1–Q3 are hard constraints; Q4 can only reduce what Q1–Q3 allowed, never enlarge it.

AUTOMATIC REJECTION AT S5

Any sentence asserting that the system is unhackable, impossible to intercept, universally quantum-secure, information-theoretically secure at system level, satellite-ready, space-proven or operational. Any sentence using secure, resilient, autonomous, high-precision, low-latency or quantum-secure as an unqualified descriptor rather than naming the measured quantity, the assumption set and the evidence level. Any numeric performance target not traceable to a measurement record. Any geopolitical, sovereign-capability, national-security or defence framing. Any statement placing Akasha-Q as a subsystem of Visvambhara, or presenting Visvambhara as the reason Akasha-Q exists. Any statement making external capital, partnership or government collaboration a precondition of the programme continuing.

REVIEW COMPOSITION AND INDEPENDENCE

  • Quorum: three seated reviewers plus the custodian, who convenes, records and does not vote.
  • Standing seat: L9 systems engineering & safety governance holds one seat at every sitting.
  • Independence requirement: at least one seated reviewer must have authored no artefact in the claim's trace and must not sit in the claim author's reporting line. If no such person is available, the only lawful outcome is defer — the sitting does not proceed on the strength of goodwill.
  • Navigator separation: the S3 navigator is never an author of the trace and never the chair. The navigator's finding on navigability is binding and is not a vote.
  • Recusal: the claim author may attend, must answer questions, and may not vote or chair.
  • L8 is advisory only: AI-assisted analysis may submit written notes pointing reviewers at artefacts or at internal inconsistencies. It holds no seat, casts no vote, and may never be cited as a supporting artefact for the truth of a claim. Its notes are recorded as advisory in the disposition.
  • Blocking: a single reject at S5 blocks approval of that sentence; approval at S4 requires no dissent on entailment, with any dissent recorded verbatim in the disposition.

PARAPHRASE IS AN UNAPPROVED CLAIM

RULE

Approval attaches to a sentence, not to a subject. Any restatement of an approved claim — a shortened slide title, a translation, a spoken answer that compresses the qualifier, a caption, a summary in an email — is a new and unapproved claim, and using it is a gate violation regardless of whether the speaker believes it means the same thing. The reason is mechanical: the qualifier and the evidence level are the parts a paraphrase drops first, and dropping them converts a bounded statement into a prohibited one.

Operationally, every person who speaks for the programme carries the approved-sentence card for the surface they are on: the exact approved sentences, their chips, and their bound qualifiers. Off-card questions have one approved answer pattern — name the metric, state that it is undetermined, and state how a target will be established from measurement. Refusal is a permitted output, not a failure of the speaker (DC-3). After every sitting and every demonstration, off-card questions are logged and enter the gate at S1 as candidate claims.

RECORD AND EXPIRY

Every disposition — including reject and defer — is written to the L6 Evidence & Provenance Fabric with the submission form, the panel roster, the independence finding, dissent, and the bound text (DC-5). Approvals expire at the next programme review unless renewed, and lapse immediately on any level fall against a claim in their trace: degradation does not wait for a meeting, and restoration runs the full sequence (DC-4).
