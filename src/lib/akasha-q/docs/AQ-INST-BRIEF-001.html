AQ-INST-BRIEF-001 — Institutional briefing: structure and inspection set

1. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE

This document defines the structure of an institutional briefing on Akasha-Q — a briefing given to a university group, a research institute, a standards or metrology body, or a technical review panel. Its audience can read a method and will ask for the assumption behind a number. It is therefore longer, slower and more falsifiable than the executive briefing (AQ-CEO-BRIEF-001), and it ends not in a decision but in the release of an inspection set.

The institutional case for Akasha-Q rests on exactly two things: the research value of the questions being asked, and the governance quality of how answers will be admitted as evidence. No third argument is permitted.

STATE DECLARED IN SECTION 3, EVERY TIME

Nothing has been built. No Akasha-Q element has reached Lab validation. Sections 4 through 8 describe an intended design and a defined process; they contain no achieved results, and the briefing is defective if any sentence implies otherwise.

4. HARD CONSTRAINT ON FRAMING

NO STRATEGIC, SOVEREIGN OR DEFENCE FRAMING Deny

The institutional case is made on research value and governance quality alone. No section, answer, annex or handout may contain geopolitical, sovereign-capability, national-security or defence framing — including when a counterpart introduces it first, invites it, or offers it as the obvious reason the work matters. An invitation to that frame is declined in one sentence, the research question is restated, and the briefing continues from the section it was in. The invitation and the decline are both written into the delivery note.

The same constraint bars the claims: unhackable, impossible to intercept, universally quantum-secure, information-theoretically secure at system level, satellite-ready, space-proven, operational. The words secure, resilient, autonomous, high-precision and low-latency are never used as bare descriptors; each is replaced by the measured quantity with its assumption set and evidence level, or by an explicit statement that the metric is undefined. Where a performance target would go, the metric is named together with the procedure that will later establish its target from measurement.

5. FUNDING POSITION AS STATED IN SECTION 10

Akasha-Q is funded by EduRankAI revenue and disciplined reinvestment. External capital, institutional partnership and government collaboration are treated as accelerants: each is described by the reach rung it would bring forward, the metric it would allow an independent party to measure rather than the programme itself, or the layer it would staff sooner. None is a precondition, a gate or a dependency, and no section of this briefing may be delivered in a way that implies the work pauses without it.

What the programme asks of an institution is therefore narrow and specific: read the inspection set, attempt to refute the design, and let the finding — supportive or not, unanimous or dissenting — be entered into the review record where the next reviewer will see it.
