AQ-SDD-001 — Scientific Demonstration Dossier: Template and Completion Rules

1. PURPOSE AND STANDING

The Scientific Demonstration Dossier (SDD) is the record produced for every demonstration run in the Akasha-Q programme, from the first optical-bench measurement on rung 1 of the reach ladder through to any later run on a dynamic platform. One run produces exactly one dossier. A dossier records a question asked under declared conditions; it does not record an outcome the programme hoped for.

No element of Akasha-Q has been built and nothing has reached laboratory validation. Every dossier that exists today therefore stands at Concept or Theory, and no dossier may assert a higher Part 12 evidence level until a sealed run has been executed and independently reviewed under these rules. The dossier is the only route by which an article, a layer or a design commitment moves up the evidence ladder. A claim that cannot be traced to a sealed dossier is not a programme claim. Dossiers are held in the restricted-access record and carry a release decision in section 17; they are not published by default.

2. THE PRE-REGISTRATION BOUNDARY

SEAL RULE

Sections 1 to 9 are written, reviewed and sealed before the first data acquisition of the run. The seal is a hash over the text of sections 1 to 9 written into the Evidence Fabric with a timestamp. Sections 10 to 17 may only be authored after the run has ended. Any edit to sections 1 to 9 after the seal voids the dossier: it is marked VOID, retained, and replaced by a new dossier identifier carrying a supersedes link. Re-running after a voided seal is a new run with a new run identifier.

The boundary exists because the failure mode this programme most needs to prevent is not a bad measurement — it is a criterion adjusted after the data arrives. Section 9 fixes what would count as success and what would count as failure while both answers are still equally unwelcome.

5. SECTION 13 IS MANDATORY

NO EMPTY SECTION 13

A dossier submitted with section 13 blank is rejected without review. “None observed” is permitted only with a written detection-coverage justification: which failure modes among F-1 to F-14 the instrumentation of this run could have detected, at what sensitivity, over what fraction of the run, and why their absence is therefore informative rather than merely unrecorded. The justification is signed by the reviewer, not the author. An article that never produces failure observations is more often under-instrumented than well behaved. DC-2 applies to the dossier as it applies to the system: absence is a value, and it never defaults to benign.

6. TAGGING AND CLAIM DISCIPLINE

  • DC-1 in the dossier. Every number in sections 12 and 14 carries MEASURED, DERIVED or MODELLED. A modelled value beside a measured one without its tag is a rejection, not a formatting defect.
  • DC-3 in scoring. An abstain outcome is a permitted system behaviour and is never scored as a run failure by itself. It is recorded in section 13 and must carry the input condition that produced it.
  • DC-4 in interpretation. A trust state that fell during the run cannot be described as recovered unless the full recovery sequence appears in the record. Section 14 may not narrate a return to NORMAL that the Evidence Fabric does not show.
  • DC-5 as an acceptance test, not an aspiration. Reconstruction is scored by a reviewer replaying the consequential transitions from the Evidence Fabric alone, without console logs and without asking the operator.
  • Prohibited in every section: unhackable, impossible to intercept, universally quantum-secure, information-theoretically secure at system level, satellite-ready, space-proven, operational. Also prohibited: the words secure, resilient, autonomous, high-precision, low-latency and quantum-secure used as bare adjectives. Name the measured quantity and its assumption set, or state that the metric is undefined.
  • No target invention. Where section 9 has no threshold, it says so and states the rule for deriving one later from measurement. A threshold that first appears in section 12 is a seal violation.

7. REVIEW ROLES, SEAL MECHANICS AND IDENTITY

Two reviewers are named in section 1 and both sign twice, once at seal and once at close. R1 (scientific) tests sections 3, 4, 9, 14 and 15 — that the question is answerable, the hypothesis falsifiable, the criteria decidable and the claim no larger than the data. R2 (safety and governance, L9) tests sections 6, 7, 8, 10 and 17 — abort authority, boundary markings, fault handling and disposition. Neither reviewer may be an author of the section they clear.

Dossier identifiers are of the form AQ-SDD-nnn, with a scenario suffix where a template carries worked scenarios, for example AQ-SDD-001-S1. A voided dossier keeps its identifier permanently; identifiers are never reused and never silently corrected. Where a later run supersedes an earlier one, both dossiers remain readable and the link is bidirectional, so that any claim can be walked back to the first run that supported it and forward to whatever replaced it.
